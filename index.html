02 · Learning outcomes

What students will be able to do

Speak HR

Recognize core HRM concepts, terms and practices and communicate them clearly.

Think strategically

Translate company strategy into HR policies and distinguish practices that do or do not support business goals.

Evaluate performance

Understand and evaluate performance-management and appraisal processes.

Design rewards

Identify the key logic and elements of compensation systems.

Develop people

Understand learning, development, training and feedback as connected HR processes.

Connect the system

See how recruitment, development, performance and reward work together.

03 · Enduring vs. Evolving

What will still matter when HR changes

Tools, platforms and regulations will change. The underlying logic of managing people will not.

01 · Job Analysis

Understand what the role requires

Before hiring, developing, evaluating or rewarding anyone, understand what the role does and what matters for successful performance.

02 · Person–Job / Person–Organization Fit

Predict who is likely to succeed

Methods change; validity, reliability, bias and fair prediction remain.

03 · Motivation & Engagement

Understand why people work well

Autonomy, competence, fairness, recognition and meaning are not tied to a specific technology.

04 · Strategic Alignment

Connect HR to business strategy

HR policies should follow from business strategy rather than exist separately from it.

05 · Performance Management

Goal → feedback → adjustment

The tools may change; defining performance, giving feedback and correcting course remain.

06 · Reward Logic

Balance fairness, competitiveness and performance

Internal equity, external competitiveness and the link between pay and performance remain fundamental.

07 · Human Judgment

Think critically, communicate, defend decisions

As AI generates more options, evaluation and justification become even more important.

Evolving knowledge

What will change faster

Teach these as current practice, not permanent truth.

05 · AI contract

Three modes of AI use

No AI

Independent mastery

In-class exercises, quizzes and exam questions.

Limited + disclosed

Brainstorming, drafting and checking may be supported by AI; final analysis and judgment remain the student's own and AI contribution is disclosed.

AI required

AI as a professional tool

Selected capstone tasks use AI for an initial draft; grading focuses on critique, revision and justification.
